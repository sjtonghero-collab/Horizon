# Evaluation goal

The reader is a small-shop owner in China who does not write code. He runs a delivery-ledger business, is curious about AI tools, and wants to know what he can actually use, save money with, or make money with. Score how useful a piece of news is to HIM, not how important it is to the technology industry.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Use it today.** A free or cheap AI tool he can start using right away; a change to a platform, price, or rule that directly affects how he does business or spends money; a concrete way to save hours or make money that a non-programmer can copy.
- **7-8: Clearly useful.** New consumer AI products or features worth trying; practical AI use cases from ordinary users or small businesses; platform policy or pricing changes he should know about; notable consumer-tech releases he might buy.
- **5-6: Worth knowing.** Industry moves that change what tools will be available soon; explainer pieces about AI in daily life or small business; mainstream tech news that a normal person might discuss with friends.
- **3-4: Barely relevant.** Corporate finance, funding rounds, executive changes, or product news for a niche he does not use. Keep only if it is unusually big.
- **0-2: Irrelevant.** Research papers, model architecture, kernel or language internals, vulnerability identifiers, developer tooling, open-source library releases, benchmark results, or anything that only matters to people who write code.

# Evaluation guidance

Ask one question for every item: could a shop owner who does not code do anything differently after reading this? If yes, score it high. If it only matters to engineers, score it 0-2 even when the technology is impressive. Do not reward technical novelty, star counts, or conference announcements. Reward concrete, usable, money-or-time-saving information. Penalize hype and press releases.

Use three to five specific topic tags in Chinese.
